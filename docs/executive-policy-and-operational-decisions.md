# LAWMA-360 v2: Executive Decisions Requiring Approval

**Status:** Decision brief for review, not approved policy.  
**Purpose:** Separate executive choices from engineering design and propose reversible defaults for the simulation and subsequent pilot.  
**Source basis:** Existing `docs/PRD.md`, `docs/api-contracts.md`, `docs/LAWMA-360-Module-Event-Flow-and-SPOF-Matrix.md`, `docs/deep-research-engineering-fixes-and-safeguards.md`, and the module specifications. Numerical thresholds and legal interpretations below are proposals unless explicitly confirmed by an authorized source. Do not present them as verified Lagos policy or operational fact.

## Executive summary

The six engineering safeguards are implementable only after policy owners decide how operational exceptions affect service, evidence, and money. The recommended path is to approve a **simulation default set** now, validate it with LAWMA, PSPs, councils, LASEPA, LASEMA, LIRS, legal counsel, and facility operators, then approve production rules separately. Simulation approval must not be treated as statutory authority.

## Decisions needing executive approval

| ID | Decision to make | Proposed default for simulation / pilot | Main tradeoff | Accountable approval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | Who bears arrears when a tenant leaves, and can debt transfer to a new occupant? | Bill each verified dwelling unit separately. Never transfer a prior occupant's balance to a successor. Put disputed vacancy balances in review; any property-level recovery requires explicit legal authority and notice. | Protects innocent tenants and service continuity; increases reconciliation and owner-level collection effort. | LAWMA commercial/revenue lead, Ministry of Justice, tenancy/legal policy owner |
| D-02 | What counts as a valid weighbridge record during loss of network or scale failure? | Network outage alone: issue locally signed, sequenced digital tickets and reconcile when online. Failed/uncalibrated scale: do not treat estimated tonnage as verified; quarantine settlement and use an authorized, auditable exception process with dual approval. | Avoids unsafe gate shutdowns without converting estimates into unquestioned payable tonnage; can delay settlement. | LAWMA operations, metrology/Weights & Measures authority, finance/audit |
| D-03 | Who can order a diversion and who pays incremental cost? | Authorized dispatch controller may issue a logged diversion only to an approved, open destination. Record reason, route, distance, acceptance and actual cost. Reimburse verified incremental costs under an approved rate card; no automatic fines in the prototype. | Improves facility resilience; adds cost and depends on current capacity, access and rate-card data. | LAWMA dispatch, facility operators, PSP representatives, finance |
| D-04 | How are flood/road hazards declared, and what service promise applies? | Safety stop requires corroborating evidence and named reviewer; keep citizen reports open, publish affected area and next update, and set a time-bounded re-service target. No automatic penalty waiver or fixed grace duration until SLA and emergency authorities approve. | Protects crews and creates an explainable process; human review is slower and may be inconsistent without clear rules. | LAWMA service quality, LASEMA, relevant environment authority, PSPs |
| D-05 | How are shortfalls allocated across tax, PSP operations, regulatory fees and council allocations? | Never silently prorate or reorder funds. Show available cash, verified obligations, shortfall, holds and proposed waterfall. Pause disbursement pending an approved waterfall and treasury/revenue validation. | Safest legally and financially; delays payments during a shortfall. | State Treasury, LIRS, LAWMA finance, council representatives, legal counsel |
| D-06 | Who may certify digital evidence, and what makes an evidence package admissible? | Preserve original evidence, hashes, timestamps, device/source identity, custody history, correction history and export log. Generate a draft certificate/checklist only; require authorized human review and legal approval before filing. | Better integrity and traceability; cannot guarantee admissibility or replace statutory certification. | LAWMA legal, Ministry of Justice, judiciary liaison, information-security owner |
| D-07 | What happens to an operator's payment after a citizen complaint? | Complaint opens a case, not an automatic full invoice freeze. Hold only a specifically identified disputed amount if authorized by the approved contract; disclose amount, reason, evidence and appeal route. | Preserves due process and cash-flow clarity; depends on contract and consumer-protection review. | LAWMA customer service, PSP contract owner, consumer-protection/legal lead |
| D-08 | What role can informal or micro-operators play? | Simulate registered feeder partners delivering to designated hubs, with measured acceptance and traceable handoff. Keep permissions, safety requirements, compensation and enforcement as configurable policy, not assumed fact. | Extends last-mile coverage; requires hub capacity, occupational safeguards and a viable contracting model. | LAWMA operations, local councils, community representatives, legal/procurement |
| D-09 | How are hazardous and medical loads handled at intake? | Stop normal processing on credible hazard alert; isolate safely, notify designated responders, preserve chain of custody and require an authorized disposition. No automatic fine, prosecution, or facility sealing without approved process. | Prioritizes worker and environmental safety; may disrupt intake and needs trained staff and safe quarantine capacity. | LASEPA, LAWMA, health authority, facility safety lead, legal counsel |
| D-10 | How are market service obligations and fleet access handled? | Treat market clean-up windows, bulk billing and toll/route exemptions as contract-specific settings. In the simulation, show the named authority, validity period, evidence and exception path for each rule. | Makes the workflow realistic without inventing universal exemptions; requires agreements with unions, councils and toll operators. | Market authority, LAWMA, relevant transport/toll operator, finance/legal |

## Approval and verification gates

1. **Policy gate:** Name a decision owner and approving authority for each row; approve the rule, effective date, geographic scope, appeal path and audit requirement in writing.
2. **Evidence gate:** Legal and agency leads verify every statutory citation, delegated power, tax treatment, data-sharing basis, and court/evidence procedure before it becomes a production rule.
3. **Operational gate:** Validate facility opening/capacity, accepted waste streams, calibration and backup procedures, route permissions, response staffing, rate cards, and hazard contacts with the responsible operators.
4. **Financial gate:** Finance and Treasury approve tariff inputs, tax treatment, account custody, settlement order, reserve funding, reconciliation and failed-payment handling. No proposed percentage or reserve amount is a default entitlement.
5. **Pilot gate:** Use synthetic or clearly labeled sample data until written approvals and field validation exist. Record every exception and retain human override authority.

## Items explicitly not approved by this brief

The earlier research documents contain specific values and claims, including percentage splits, tolerance bands, time windows, penalties, surcharge amounts, reserve amounts, fine/conviction estimates, and statutory interpretations. Treat each as an **unverified proposal**, not an approved threshold or fact. Confirm against executed contracts, current laws and regulations, agency directives, audited operational data, and written executive decisions before implementation.

## Decision record template

For each D-ID, record: decision; selected option; scope; effective date; approving person/office; legal/contract reference; data source; exception and appeal process; review date; and implementation owner. Unresolved decisions remain configurable and must not trigger irreversible payments, penalties, denial of service, or prosecution.
